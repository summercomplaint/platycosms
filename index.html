The ten platycosms

A platycosm is a possible shape for a finite universe that is flat: every small region looks exactly like ordinary space, but go far enough in a straight line and you come back to where you started. Up to the choice of sizes there are exactly ten of them. Six are orientable; in the other four, a round trip can bring you back as your own mirror image. The names are Conway and Rossetti's.

How to read the pictures. Each space is drawn as one cell, a box whose faces are glued in pairs: walk out through one face and you walk in through its partner. Tubes of the same colour are the same edge of the space, and the arrowheads show which way round they are glued. Balls of the same colour are the same point. The crab shows how you arrive: shifted, turned, or mirrored (watch the red claw and the R on its back).
